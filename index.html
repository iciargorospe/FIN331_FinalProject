Did academic research kill the anomalies?

Do investment strategy returns decline after academic publication?

01

What is an anomaly?

An anomaly is a pattern in stock returns that seems difficult to explain with standard financial theory. Researchers turn these patterns into investment strategies and test whether they persist.

A long-short return is the return from holding stocks expected to do well while shorting stocks expected to do poorly. It is the difference between the two sides, reported here as a monthly percentage. If that difference shrinks after a strategy is published, it may be consistent with investors trading it away. It does not tell us why it shrank.

↳Publication can make an idea more visible. Whether visibility changes returns is the question, not an assumption.

Post-publication

After

03

Preliminary Results

In our five-anomaly prototype, all five strategies have lower average monthly returns after publication than during their original research samples.

These are descriptive findings, not evidence that publication caused the declines. Our final analysis will expand the sample and test these differences more carefully.

05

Read this as a prototype

Five-anomaly prototype · Not final results

This explorer covers only Book-to-Market, Initial Public Offerings, 12-Month Momentum, Accruals, and Asset Growth. It is a descriptive comparison, not a causal test. A change after publication does not prove publication caused the change.

Returns may also reflect changing market conditions, trading costs, implementation choices, or data updates. We do not adjust for these factors here, and the original sample dates are metadata estimates rather than a common evaluation window.
