Did academic research kill the anomalies?

Do investment strategy returns decline after academic publication?

01

What is an anomaly?

An anomaly is a pattern in stock returns that seems difficult to explain with standard financial theory. Researchers turn these patterns into investment strategies and test whether they persist.

A long-short return is the return from holding stocks expected to do well while shorting stocks expected to do poorly. It is the difference between the two sides, reported here as a monthly percentage. If that difference shrinks after a strategy is published, it may be consistent with investors trading it away. It does not tell us why it shrank.

↳Publication can make an idea more visible. Whether visibility changes returns is the question, not an assumption.

03

Full-Sample Results

Across all eligible anomalies, average monthly long-short returns were lower after publication than in the original research samples. Returns declined overall, but this is descriptive evidence rather than a causal finding.

In our preferred panel regression, the broad decline remains statistically significant. It does not detect an additional statistically significant decline specifically after publication. We do not claim that publication caused the observed declines.

07

Limitations

Full-sample results · Associational evidence

The project uses only anomalies meeting the saved eligibility rule. Publication year observations and observations before the original research sample are excluded because exact publication dates and common evaluation windows are unavailable.

Original-sample selection, different portfolio implementations, changing market conditions, data updates, and implementation choices may affect returns. Category comparisons are exploratory. Trading-cost scenarios are hypothetical; we have not directly tested actual trading difficulty or combined a direct trading-cost dataset with these returns.

Lower returns overall do not establish that publication caused the decline. In particular, the preferred panel regression identifies no additional statistically significant decline specifically after publication.
